
### Categorizing dimensions of inequality 

The subgroup composition describes which individuals are grouped together based on their profiles.  There are several factors to take into consideration when **deciding the best option for categorizing subgroups.**
#### Consideration 1: Granularity
**Granularity of the available information**. More granular (:more detailed) information allows for a greater range of options for categorizing subgroups. 

#### Consideration 2: Sample Size
**Consequences** of having a **small sample size**: 
 - resulting estimate is not reliable. 
 - further analysis is limited.
 - In some cases, may pose a risk to confidentiality. Individual with unique or rare characteristics may be identifiable. 

**Addressing a small sample size**: 
 - Calculating the standard error or confidence intervals are helpful reliability of estimates. 
 - The Demographic and Health Surveys (DHS) requires to flag estimates from sample sizes ranging between 25-49 individuals. 
 - Drop estimate
 - Recategorizing the inequality dimension by combining data from multiple responses that consequently has a larger sample size.  

#### Consideration 3: Resolution issue
Generally, a smaller number of subgroups for a dimension of inequality will capture less heterogeneity (or variation) between the subgroups.
