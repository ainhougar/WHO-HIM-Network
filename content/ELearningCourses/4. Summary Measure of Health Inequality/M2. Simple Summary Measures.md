
### Favorable and adverse health indicators
There are favorable and adverse health indicators.  Certain indicators, however, present a more nuanced situation, where the health indicator is not obviously favourable or adverse. The desired situation is neither the maximum nor the minimum. 

### Difference and ratio 
#### Difference
They make pairwise comparisons. In cases where there are more than two subgroups, we can use data from two selected subgroups. 

- For **favourable health indicators**, the difference is calculated as the advantaged subgroup estimate minus the disadvantaged subgroup estimate.  
- For **adverse health indicators**, the difference is calculated as the disadvantaged subgroup estimate minus the advantaged subgroup estimate.  


For **non-ordered dimensions** consisting of more than two subgroups, difference is usually calculated as the **highest minus the lowest subgroup estimate**, regardless of the indicator type.

#### Ratio

**Ratio** is a measure of relative inequality. It is multiplicative, showing how much better (or worse) one subgroup is doing in relation to the other. Ratio values are unitless.
