

Complex summary measures of health inequality are calculated using information about **all subgroups of a population**. They are appropriate to use with dimensions of inequality comprised of more than two subgroups.  This decision chart can be used as a quick guide for determining **which common complex summary measures of health inequality** are appropriate for use in **different applications**.

### Some complex summary measures 

**Mean difference from best group** expresses the absolute mean difference between each subgroup and the reference subgroup with the most favourable estimate.


**Population attributable risk**: the absolute improvement in a setting average (such as national average) that could be achieved if all population subgroups had the same level of health as a reference point.

**Population attributable fraction**: Expresses the same information as PAR in relative terms. Calculated by dividing PAR by setting average and multiplying by 100. 


### HEAT & HEAT Plus 

Key tools that aid with calculation of complex summary measures are integrated in the WHO Health Equity Assessment Toolkit. 
- HEAT, Built-in Database Edition: comes with preinstalled datasets consisting of all disaggregated data in the WHO Health Inequality Data Repository
- HEAT Plus: enables users to upload and work with their own database
