
## Scope
The scope generally refers to:
- The latest status reported
	- Reporting the latest status of inequality is important. 
- Change over time 
	- Compares the latest status from data from previous period(s). 
	- Is the situation improving? Staying the same? 
- Benchmarking
	- Compares the level of inequality across similar settings (i.e: economic related inequality across countries/across provinces  )
	- Requires that the data are reasonably comparable (i.e: collected in the same time period, using similar methods)


## Technical Content

Specific results that support finding and conclusions: 
- Disaggregated data 
- Summary measures 
- Technical details 
- Information about metadata and methods

Technical content needs to be selected depending on the audience. It needs to always be transparent, accurate and easy to understand for the audience. 

The technical content presented depends on the reporting format chosen: 
- Journal articles focus on detailed methods and results 
- Policy-briefs have a greater focus on the applications and use of findings. 

When selecting technical content, **disaggregated data is a good starting point** because: 
- Pattern description across population are straightforward/intuitive and therefore appropriate for diverse audiences. 

**Summary measures** should only be presented if they enhance the impact of reporting. Some of the advantages of summary measures are: 
- a single number to describe inequality 
- quick for comparisons
Generally, it is good to accompany summary measures with disaggregated data.

Other **key elements** to contextualize findings : 
- Overage average of the health indicator

- population share of those affected 
- uncertainty measures
