## Background and context 
Background and context **situates** the purpose of monitoring. Questions such as: 
- Why was monitoring undertaken? 
- What is monitoring setting out to accomplish? 

## Methods
Discussing methods and approaches is important to understand how the data supports or does not support certain conclusions. In this step, the parameters, their limitations, suitability, and excluded alternatives are described. (The depth and level of detail depends on the audience.). In journal article the level of detail needs to be sufficient such that the presented analysis could be replicated by third-parties. 

## Findings and conclusions
Generally results are presented going from a wide scope into a narrower focus. Findings should be complemented by implications. 

### Best practices: 

- When **simple and complex summary measures** support the same conclusion, it is preferable to just report the simple summary measures. When there are discrepancies, complex measures can be presented if the audience has technical knowledge, otherwise, explaining the reason of the discrepancy is recommended. 

-  Both absolute and relative summary measures, overall average and disagregated data should be reported. 

- Reporting population share is important, especially if the size of subgroups are varied. 
- Report national avrages alongside disaggregated data 
- Consider whether reporting statistical significance is appropriate (shows a difference that is meaningful from a public health perspective). /!\ : careful with small differences that have statistical significance because they are based of large sample sizes. Careful with making false conclusions that are produced from statistical constructs.
