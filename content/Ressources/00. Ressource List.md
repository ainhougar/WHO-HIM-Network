
WHO has developed numerous resources to support health inequality monitoring, which are all accessible through the Health Inequality Monitor website. We will detail some of these ressources in this section. 

#### Skill building ressources 

There are various E-Learning courses that help build quantitative analysis skills. 
Some courses teach how to conduct **data disaggregation** depending on the software: 
- [Stata](https://thegraphcourses.org/courses/inequality-analysis-using-stata-disaggregated-data-from-surveys/](https://thegraphcourses.org/courses/inequality-analysis-using-stata-disaggregated-data-from-surveys/) )
- [R software](https://thegraphcourses.org/courses/inequality-analysis-using-r-disaggregated-data-from-surveys/](https://thegraphcourses.org/courses/inequality-analysis-using-r-disaggregated-data-from-surveys/) 
- [Excel](https://thegraphcourses.org/courses/inequality-analysis-using-excel-disaggregated-data/)

There is a course on how to use the Health Equity Assessment Toolkit (HEAT and HEAT Plus) developed by WHO: [course](https://thegraphcourses.org/courses/health-equity-assessment-toolkit-heat-and-heat-plus/) . 
HEAT and HEAT Plus are **interactive applications** that allow users **to explore patterns of inequality** in the WHO health inequality data repository. Sources of data included are household surveys such as, DHS and MICS, or other external sources such as Eurodata. Heat and Heat Plus can interactively disaggregate data, calculate summary measures of inequality, compare inequality between settings, and create and export customized results. 

HEAT can be explored here: [link](http://www.who.int/data/inequality-monitor/assessment-toolkit).  
Youtube tutorial on how to navigate around HEAT:[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_MMbZe5a1_o) 

### Hanbooks, manuals, reports 

 - Book, *Harnessing data to advance health equity*, [link](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/7ed039ab-4a7f-4f66-9b4b-264008d98f81/content). 
	 This is a book that compiles detailed information on health inequality monitoring. Part 2 in this book, *Generating Impact*, is not directly covered in the E-Learning course. Part 4, *Analysis and Reporting*, discusses many technical elements and calculating measures and interpreting data. This chapter may be worth reviewing indefinite times. 

- Manual, a step-by-step manual to National Health Inequality Monitoring: [link](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/33aecaf3-a336-4a65-9f30-2ae3ee4f9213/content). 
	This manual describes the steps to monitoring that are described in the E-Learning Courses. The structure is the same. It is an interesting source if we want to delve into some detail on the sections.
